# AI-Assisted Contribution Provenance Policy

- Status: Active
- Version: 1.0
- Owner: NAEOS Foundation

## 1. Purpose

AI-assisted and agent-generated material (code, documentation, prompts,
specifications) is welcome in NAEOS. The purpose of this policy is to keep
contributions legally clean when the production of code is increasingly
automated, without discouraging tool use.

## 2. Principles

1. Ownership is about rights, not about who typed the text. A contributor must
   have the right to submit every line of a contribution, whether it was typed,
   pasted, or generated by an AI tool.
2. Every contribution — human- or AI-generated — must respect third-party
   copyrights and licenses. Never commit content whose provenance is unclear or
   that you do not have the right to redistribute.
3. The DCO (`Signed-off-by`) remains the mechanism for certifying authorship
   and submission rights, regardless of how the content was produced.
4. Secrets, credentials, personal data, and confidential customer material must
   never be committed, whether they entered the tree by hand or by tool.

## 3. Provenance Classification

For material in the repository, reviewers may assign one of:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| KNOWN | Human-authored, or tool-assisted with an honest record; contributor holds the rights |
| POSSIBLE | Tool-generated with unverified tool/output terms, or source material is unclear |
| UNKNOWN | No contributor can explain or vouch for the material's origin |

Material classified POSSIBLE or UNKNOWN and believed to contain third-party or
restricted content must be flagged for review rather than committed silently.

## 4. Documenting AI Assistance

Disclosure is recommended, not mandatory:

- Large agent-generated PRs: add a `Co-authored-by: <tool> <package>` trailer
  to commits, or note the tool and prompt class in the PR description.
- Repo files that are wholesale generated (e.g., docs, scaffolds, benchmarks):
  a file-level `# Generated with <tool> — <date>` header is helpful.
- Journals, conferences, or regulators that require AI disclosure: follow their
  requirements; this policy does not override them.

## 5. What Reviewers Do

- Reviewers apply judgment, not prohibition. A commit with correct DCO and
  clean provenance is accepted whether or not it mentions a tool.
- When a PR shows heavy bulk generation (hundreds of files, templated wording),
  maintainers may ask for a provenance note.
- Anything that looks like copied third-party material is routed to the normal
  attribution review process (see `NOTICE` for attribution conventions).

## 6. Enforcement and Scope

This policy applies to all repositories owned by the NAEOS Foundation.
Secrets and provenance issues found in PR review are treated as blocking;
failure to disclose is handled by maintainer discretion, matching severity.
